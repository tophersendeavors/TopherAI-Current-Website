The short answer

An AI receptionist can support initial fact gathering and call routing for a law firm. Start with approved questions, a written record, and a clear next action. Confirm coverage, booking, and record delivery for your setup in a demo.

Your team keeps responsibility for conflict decisions, legal advice, deadline assessment, and whether to accept a matter.

Where firms lose matters they already paid for

Review the journey from a new inquiry to a consultation. Start with your own call records, callback times, and follow-up outcomes.

Check these four parts of your intake process:

  • The call arrives while everyone is unavailable. Depositions, hearings, client meetings, lunch. Check whether the caller receives a timely callback.
  • After-hours volume is invisible. Separate after-hours inquiries from calls during office hours to understand when coverage is needed.
  • Screening is inconsistent. Check completed records for missing contact details, dates, and answers to your approved questions.
  • Follow-up depends on someone's memory. Assign each inquiry to a person and track whether the next action was completed.

What Topher does on a legal call

Plan call coverage

Confirm business-hours and after-hours routing, call limits, and the fallback when a transfer is unavailable.

Screens the matter

Case type, date of incident, jurisdiction, injuries or charges, insurance, whether they've retained counsel, and any deadlines the caller mentions for attorney review.

Collects information for conflict review

Decide when to collect opposing party names and who reviews them. Automated collection does not establish conflict clearance.

Books the right attorney

Confirm attorney routing, calendar compatibility, consultation length, and booking rules in the demo.

Routes exceptions for review

Approve the response for out-of-scope inquiries, requests for legal advice, and callers who need to speak to a person.

Delivers the written intake

Review a sample record for field accuracy, unknown answers, assigned owner, and next action. Confirm where the record is delivered.

Detailed walkthrough of the screening logic: AI Client Intake for Law Firms.

The intake workflow, end to end

1

Call answered and reason captured

One open question — "tell me what happened" — and the caller explains. No menu, no hold music, no transfer.

2

Matter type identified

The reasoning layer maps the narrative to a practice area, including when the caller's own framing is wrong about what kind of case they have.

3

Practice-specific screening

Your criteria for that matter type, asked conversationally and skipping anything already covered.

4

Dates and parties recorded for review

Record party names and caller-stated dates for the firm to review. Do not calculate legal deadlines or promise conflict clearance.

5

Consultation booked or matter declined

Qualified callers get a real appointment with the right attorney and a confirmation. Declined callers get a respectful explanation and a referral direction.

6

Record filed and team notified

Confirm the record destination, assigned reviewer, and notification route. Test urgent handoff and what happens if delivery fails.

Ethics, confidentiality, and the unauthorized-practice line

This is the first question every managing partner asks, and it deserves a direct answer rather than reassurance.

It does not give legal advice

Approve the response and handoff for questions about case merit, case value, outcomes, and legal deadlines. Keep representation decisions with the firm, and test these boundaries in the demo.

Prospective-client information is treated as confidential

Review provider terms, storage, retention, access controls, and any model-training use before sharing sensitive information. Specify which details should be collected at each stage.

Conflicts are screened before commitments

Specify who reviews party names and when a possible conflict should stop automated scheduling. The firm decides whether it can accept a matter.

Disclosure is a setting you control

Have your firm approve assistant disclosure, recording consent, and confirmation language for its applicable requirements.

Bring your firm's approved requirements to the demo. The ABA announcement of Formal Opinion 512 (July 29, 2024; accessed September 6, 2026) outlines ethical considerations including competence, confidentiality, and communication.

It fits the stack you already run

Tell us which calendar, case management system, and notification channel your firm uses. Review the requested connection and workflow with us, then verify field mapping, permissions, delivery, and failure handling before relying on it.

Questions firms ask

Your firm should review the workflow against its applicable rules and circumstances. Approve disclosure, consent, confidentiality, supervision, and legal-advice boundaries before launch.
Agree what the assistant should collect and when to pause for a person. Review provider terms, retention, access, and model-training use before collecting sensitive information.
