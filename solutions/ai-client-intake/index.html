The short answer

AI client intake is software that answers an inbound inquiry, asks the qualifying questions a trained intake coordinator would ask, and records structured answers in your system — automatically, in real time, 24 hours a day.

It replaces the gap between "someone reached out" and "someone on our team followed up." That gap is where most businesses lose the majority of the leads they paid to generate.

What AI client intake actually is

Intake is the moment a stranger becomes a prospect. Someone calls, fills a form, or sends a message, and a set of questions has to get asked: what do you need, when do you need it, does it fall within what we do, and are you a fit worth an hour of our time.

Traditionally a person asks those questions. That person is expensive, works business hours, and asks them slightly differently every time depending on how their morning went. AI client intake performs the same conversation with a voice agent — but it answers on the second ring, follows the same script on the four hundredth call as the first, and hands off a clean record instead of a sticky note.

It is not a phone tree, and it is not a chatbot bolted to your website. It is a conversation. The caller talks the way they normally talk, gets interrupted, changes their mind, mentions three things at once — and the system follows.

Why intake breaks in almost every business

Not because anyone is careless. Because intake demands instant availability from people who have other jobs.

1 in 4inbound calls to small businesses go unanswered

The failure is structural, and it shows up the same way everywhere:

  • Calls arrive when nobody can take them. Lunch, court, a treatment room, a jobsite, 7pm on a Friday. A voicemail is a lead asking to be ignored.
  • The intake questions get skipped under pressure. Whoever picks up is mid-task, so they take a name and a number and promise a callback.
  • The callback happens hours later. By then the prospect has called two competitors and booked with one.
  • The record is inconsistent. Three staff members capture three different sets of fields, so nobody can tell which marketing spend produced which client.

Every one of these is a timing problem, not a talent problem — which is exactly the kind of problem software solves well.

How it works

1

The call is answered instantly

Topher answers on your existing number — as the primary line, as overflow when your team is busy, or after hours only. You decide which.

2

It listens for the real reason they called

Open-ended, not a menu. The caller explains their situation in their own words, and the reasoning layer identifies what they actually need — including what they're worried about but haven't said directly.

3

It asks your qualifying questions

The ones you wrote, in the order you want, adapted to what the caller already told you. Nobody gets asked a question they just answered.

4

It routes on the answer

Qualified and urgent gets warm-transferred to a human immediately. Qualified and routine gets booked. Out of scope gets a polite, accurate explanation and a referral — which protects your team's calendar as much as the bookings do.

5

It books against real availability

Live calendar, live rules. Buffer times, consultation lengths, who handles which matter type. The confirmation goes out before the call ends.

6

It writes the record and tells your team

Structured fields, full transcript, a summary a human can read in fifteen seconds, and the recommended next action — in your CRM, your inbox, or both.

What it captures

The specifics are yours to define. Most intake configurations collect some version of this:

Identity and reach

Name, callback number, email, preferred contact method and time. Spelled back and confirmed, so the callback actually connects.

The need, in their words

What happened, what they want, and the language they used — the part that gets lost when someone types a summary from memory.

Qualification

Timeline, budget range, location or jurisdiction, insurance or payment method, whether they're already working with someone.

Source and urgency

How they found you and how fast they need an answer — the two fields that make your marketing reports true.

Where the data goes

Intake is only worth doing if the record lands somewhere your team already works. Topher writes to the systems you use — CRMs and case management platforms, calendars, email and SMS, and anything reachable by webhook or Zapier. If a system has an API, the record goes into it; if it doesn't, the record still arrives as a structured summary in your inbox.

What arrives after every call: the caller's details, the qualification answers as named fields, the full transcript, a short summary, a recommended next action, and the recording.

What it returns

The math on intake is unusually simple, because you only need to know two numbers: how many inquiries you currently miss, and what one client is worth.

A firm taking 200 calls a month that misses 25% of them is losing 50 conversations. If one in ten would have become a client, and a client is worth $3,000, that's $15,000 of monthly revenue leaving through an unanswered phone — against a cost that doesn't move whether the volume is 200 calls or 400.
