How to calculate the cost of missed calls

Published October 1, 2026 · For businesses that receive customer inquiries by phone

A missed call is a potential opportunity, but it is not automatically a lost sale. Estimate its value using your own new-customer inquiries, conversion rate, and average sale value. Keep existing-customer calls, spam, repeat attempts, and calls you successfully return separate.

The missed-call revenue formula

Potential monthly revenue = missed calls × share of new-customer inquiries × inquiry-to-sale conversion rate × average first-sale value.

For example, 40 missed calls × 50% new-customer inquiries × 20% conversion × $300 per sale = $1,200 in potential monthly gross revenue. These numbers are an illustration, not a benchmark or a prediction for your business.

Get usable numbers from your call records

  1. Choose a fixed period, such as the last full month. Count unanswered calls during business hours and after hours.
  2. Deduplicate repeated attempts from the same person. Exclude spam and calls unrelated to buying your service.
  3. Review callback outcomes. A caller who later books or buys should not be counted as a lost opportunity.
  4. Calculate conversion using a comparable group of answered new-customer inquiries. Use completed sales if your revenue depends on attendance or a later decision.
  5. Use average first-sale revenue from your records. If you use lifetime value instead, label the result clearly and account for repeat purchases separately.

Revenue opportunity is different from profit

A $300 sale is not $300 in profit. To estimate a contribution toward profit, multiply the revenue estimate by your contribution margin after the costs associated with fulfilling the sale. Capacity also matters: if your calendar is full, answering more inquiries may not create additional sales.

What can you change?

Start with the part of the call process you can observe. Set a callback owner, review after-hours demand, and record whether unanswered callers are reached. If staff cannot cover the calls, compare an AI answering service with staffing or other coverage options.

For businesses that need caller details before a follow-up, review AI client intake. If callers need appointments, define availability and handoff rules for an AI receptionist. Evaluate a pilot using answered inquiries, completed appointments, sales, and operating cost—not call volume alone.

Frequently asked questions

Does every missed call cost money?

No. Some calls are repeat attempts, existing customers, spam, or inquiries that would not convert. Some are recovered through callbacks. Use your own records to estimate the portion that represents an unrecovered sales opportunity.

Can I use this for any industry?

Yes, the arithmetic works for any business that can estimate inquiry volume, conversion, and sale value. Adapt the outcome: a booked consultation is different from a paid appointment or a completed job.

How do I know whether better coverage pays for itself?

Compare the extra contribution from completed sales with the total cost of coverage during a measured pilot. Include setup, subscription or staffing costs, and time spent reviewing and following up.
